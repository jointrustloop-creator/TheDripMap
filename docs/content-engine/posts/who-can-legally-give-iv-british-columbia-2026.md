Before you book an IV drip in British Columbia, it is worth knowing who is legally allowed to put the needle in your arm. In BC, starting an IV is a regulated activity, and only specific licensed professionals can do it in a wellness setting.

This guide explains, in plain language, who can legally give you an IV in British Columbia in 2026. It is written for patients choosing a safe clinic, not for operators. It is general information, not legal or medical advice, and the rules can change. Always confirm current requirements with the relevant BC regulatory college and with the clinician in front of you. ## The short answer

In British Columbia, the people who can legally start and run a wellness IV for you are:

- **Physicians (MDs)**, regulated by the College of Physicians and Surgeons of British Columbia (CPSBC)
- **Nurse practitioners (NPs) and registered nurses (RNs)**, regulated by the British Columbia College of Nurses and Midwives (BCCNM)
- **Licensed practical nurses (LPNs)** with the right education, who can initiate and discontinue a peripheral IV within BCCNM standards
- **Naturopathic doctors (NDs)** who hold advanced IV certification from the College of Naturopaths of British Columbia (CNPBC)

Anyone who is not a regulated health professional acting within their authorized scope cannot legally start an IV on you in BC.

## Why IV therapy is regulated in BC

Under British Columbia's Health Professions Act, inserting an IV and administering substances through it is reserved to specific regulated professions. Each profession's college sets the standards and the required education. On top of that, [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products.html) governs which drugs and natural health products can be compounded or sold for IV use. So the question is always two-part: is the person authorized, and is the substance legal to administer.

## Nurses and physicians

Most IV clinics in BC run on nursing and physician credentials. Inserting a peripheral IV and administering prescribed substances is within the authorized scope of RNs and NPs regulated by the [British Columbia College of Nurses and Midwives](https://www.bccnm.ca/). NPs can also prescribe, so some BC clinics are nurse-practitioner-led.

BC is also one of the provinces where licensed practical nurses can perform aspects of IV therapy. With the appropriate education, an LPN in BC may initiate and discontinue a peripheral intravenous line within the standards BCCNM sets. The prescriber for what flows through the line is still a physician or NP.

## Naturopathic doctors and IV therapy in BC

British Columbia allows naturopathic doctors to administer IV therapy, but it is not an automatic part of a naturopathic license. An ND registered with the [College of Naturopaths of British Columbia](https://www.cnpbc.bc.ca/) may prescribe and administer IV therapy only if they hold the advanced IV certification, a specific add-on credential beyond the standard scope.

Practically, that means a naturopathic clinic offering IVs in BC should be able to confirm that the treating ND holds the advanced IV certification. It is a fair question to ask, and a well-run clinic will answer it directly.

## Who cannot legally start your IV in BC

- Aestheticians or medical-spa staff who are not also nurses or physicians
- Wellness coaches, nutritionists, or unregulated "IV technicians"
- Naturopaths without the CNPBC advanced IV certification
- Anyone performing the injection outside an appropriate order or scope

If a clinic cannot tell you the credential of the person starting your IV, treat that as a reason to pause.

## What to check before you book in BC

- **Ask who starts the IV** and their credential (RN, NP, MD, LPN within scope, or an ND with advanced IV certification).
- **Ask who prescribes or acts as medical director.**
- **Ask what is in the bag** and whether it is compounded by a licensed pharmacy.
- **Be skeptical of cure or detox claims.** IV vitamin therapy is a legitimate wellness service, not a treatment for disease.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a naturopath give you an IV in British Columbia?

Yes, but only a naturopathic doctor registered with the College of Naturopaths of British Columbia who also holds the advanced IV certification. IV therapy is not part of the default naturopathic scope in BC; it requires that specific additional credential. It is reasonable to ask a naturopathic clinic to confirm the certification before you book.

### Can an LPN give IV therapy in BC?

With the appropriate education, a licensed practical nurse in BC may initiate and discontinue a peripheral IV within BCCNM standards. The substance being infused still needs a prescriber, typically a physician or nurse practitioner. So an LPN may start your line while an NP or MD stands behind the protocol.

### Do you need a doctor on site for IV therapy in BC?

Not necessarily a physician in the room, but there must be a qualified prescriber behind the treatment. Nurse practitioners can prescribe and oversee protocols themselves, and many BC clinics are NP-led. Others use a physician medical director. What matters is that a licensed prescriber stands behind what you receive.

### Is IV vitamin therapy legal in British Columbia?

Yes. Wellness IV therapy is legal in BC when it is delivered by an authorized professional using ingredients that are legal to compound and administer. The regulation is about who performs the act and what is in the bag, not a ban on the service.

### What should I ask a clinic before my first IV in BC?

Ask who will start the IV and their credential, who prescribes or acts as medical director, what is in the drip and where it is compounded, and how they screen you for contraindications. Clear answers are a sign of the safety infrastructure a good clinic is built on.
