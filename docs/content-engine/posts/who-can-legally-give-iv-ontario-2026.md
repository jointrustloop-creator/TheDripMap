Choosing an IV therapy clinic in Ontario usually comes down to one question most people never think to ask: is the person putting the needle in your arm actually allowed to? In Ontario, starting an IV is a regulated act, and only specific licensed professionals can legally do it in a wellness setting.

This guide explains, in plain language, who can legally give you an IV in Ontario in 2026. It is written for patients choosing a safe clinic, not for operators. It is general information, not legal or medical advice, and the rules can change. Always confirm current requirements with the relevant Ontario regulatory college and with the clinician in front of you. ## The short answer

In Ontario, the people who can legally start and run a wellness IV for you are:

- **Physicians (MDs)**, regulated by the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Ontario (CPSO)
- **Nurse practitioners (NPs) and registered nurses (RNs)**, regulated by the College of Nurses of Ontario (CNO)
- **Registered practical nurses (RPNs)** with the right added competencies and an authorizing mechanism, also under the CNO
- **Naturopathic doctors (NDs)** who hold Ontario's specific intravenous authorization from the College of Naturopaths of Ontario (CONO), and only for a defined list of substances on inspected premises

Anyone who is not a regulated health professional acting within their authorized scope, a "wellness technician," an aesthetician without nursing credentials, or a health coach, cannot legally start an IV on you in Ontario.

## Why IV therapy is regulated in Ontario

Under Ontario's Regulated Health Professions Act, "administering a substance by injection or inhalation" is a controlled act. Controlled acts can only be performed by professionals whose legislation authorizes them, or by someone the authorized professional has properly delegated to. That is the legal reason a receptionist or an unregulated technician cannot simply insert an IV, even under a clinic's roof.

Federally, [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products.html) governs the drugs and natural health products that can be compounded or sold for IV use. So two layers apply at once: who is allowed to perform the act, and what is legally allowed in the bag.

## Nurses and physicians

For most IV clinics in Ontario, the person starting your drip is a nurse or physician. Inserting a peripheral IV and administering prescribed substances is within the authorized scope of RNs and NPs regulated by the [College of Nurses of Ontario](https://www.cno.org/). NPs can also prescribe, which is why some clinics are NP-led rather than requiring a separate physician on site. RPNs can perform IV therapy where they have the added competencies and an appropriate order or authorizing mechanism in place.

A physician regulated by the CPSO can both prescribe and administer, and in many wellness clinics an MD or NP acts as the prescriber or medical director while nurses run the day-to-day infusions.

## Naturopathic doctors and IV therapy in Ontario

Ontario is one of the provinces where a naturopathic doctor can legally administer certain IV substances, but the bar is specific and worth understanding. Under the Naturopathy Act, 2007, administering a substance by injection or inhalation is a controlled act authorized to naturopaths only after additional certification.

To administer IVs, an Ontario ND must pass the College's [Intravenous Infusion Therapy (IVIT)](https://www.collegeofnaturopaths.on.ca/public/ivit-inspection-program/) requirements, which include a prescribing and therapeutics examination and an IVIT examination, and they can only offer IV therapy on premises that have passed CONO's IVIT inspection. The College maintains a defined list of substances an authorized ND may administer; it is not open-ended.

The practical takeaway: a naturopathic clinic offering IVs in Ontario should be able to tell you that the treating ND holds CONO IVIT authorization and that the premises are inspected. It is a fair and reasonable question to ask.

## Who cannot legally start your IV in Ontario

- Aestheticians or "medical spa" staff who are not also nurses or physicians
- Holistic health coaches, nutritionists, or wellness consultants
- Naturopaths without CONO IVIT authorization
- Any staff member performing the injection outside a proper delegation or order

If a clinic cannot clearly tell you the credential of the person starting your IV, treat that as a reason to pause.

## What to check before you book in Ontario

- **Ask who starts the IV** and what their credential is (RN, NP, MD, or CONO-authorized ND).
- **Ask who the prescriber or medical director is.** A named MD or NP overseeing the protocols is a good sign.
- **Ask what is in the bag** and whether the ingredients are compounded by a licensed pharmacy.
- **Be skeptical of dramatic claims.** IV vitamin therapy is a legitimate wellness service, but cures, "detox," and disease-treatment promises are red flags.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a naturopath give you an IV in Ontario?

Yes, but only a naturopathic doctor who holds the College of Naturopaths of Ontario's intravenous (IVIT) authorization, and only for a defined list of substances on premises that have passed CONO's IVIT inspection. A naturopath without that authorization cannot legally start an IV. It is reasonable to ask a naturopathic clinic to confirm its IVIT authorization before you book.

### Do you need a doctor on site for IV therapy in Ontario?

Not necessarily a physician physically present, but there should be a qualified prescriber behind the treatment. Nurse practitioners can prescribe and oversee protocols themselves, and many clinics are NP-led. Others use a physician medical director who sets the protocols that on-site nurses follow. What matters is that a licensed prescriber stands behind what you receive.

### Can a registered nurse run an IV clinic in Ontario?

An RN can start and run IVs within their scope, but the substances still need a prescriber. In practice an RN-run clinic works alongside an NP or physician who prescribes and oversees the protocols. Ask who the prescriber is if it is not clear.

### Is IV vitamin therapy legal in Ontario?

Yes. Wellness IV therapy is legal in Ontario when it is delivered by an authorized professional using ingredients that are legal to compound and administer. The regulation is about who performs the act and what is in the bag, not a ban on the service itself.

### What should I ask a clinic before my first IV in Ontario?

Ask who will start the IV and their credential, who prescribes or acts as medical director, what is in the drip and where it is compounded, and how they screen you for contraindications. A clinic that answers these clearly is showing you the safety infrastructure you are paying for.
