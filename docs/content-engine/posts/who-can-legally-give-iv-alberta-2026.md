If you are booking an IV drip in Alberta, one question matters more than the menu or the price: is the person starting your IV legally allowed to? In Alberta, administering an IV is a restricted activity, and only specific licensed professionals can perform it in a wellness setting.

This guide explains, in plain language, who can legally give you an IV in Alberta in 2026. It is written for patients choosing a safe clinic, not for operators. It is general information, not legal or medical advice, and the rules can change. Always confirm current requirements with the relevant Alberta regulatory college and with the clinician in front of you. ## The short answer

In Alberta, the people who can legally start and run a wellness IV for you are:

- **Physicians (MDs)**, regulated by the College of Physicians and Surgeons of Alberta (CPSA)
- **Nurse practitioners (NPs) and registered nurses (RNs)**, regulated by the College of Registered Nurses of Alberta (CRNA)
- **Licensed practical nurses (LPNs)** with additional certification, regulated by the College of Licensed Practical Nurses of Alberta (CLPNA)
- **Naturopathic doctors (NDs)** who hold IV authorization from the College of Naturopathic Doctors of Alberta (CNDA), for a defined and narrower list of substances

Anyone who is not a regulated health professional acting within their authorized scope cannot legally start an IV on you in Alberta.

## Why IV therapy is a restricted activity in Alberta

Alberta's Health Professions Act uses the concept of "restricted activities." Inserting an instrument or fluid into the body beyond a certain point, which includes starting an IV, is a restricted activity that a professional can only perform if their college authorizes it. That is the legal reason an unregulated technician cannot start your IV, regardless of what the clinic calls the role.

Layered on top, [Health Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/drugs-health-products.html) governs the drugs and natural health products that can be compounded or administered by IV. So two questions always apply: is the person authorized for the restricted activity, and is the substance legal to administer.

## Nurses and physicians

Most Alberta IV clinics run on nursing and physician credentials. Inserting a peripheral IV is within an Alberta RN's authorized scope under the [College of Registered Nurses of Alberta](https://www.nurses.ab.ca/), and NPs have the added authority to prescribe what flows through the line. A physician regulated by the CPSA can both prescribe and administer, and in many wellness clinics an MD or NP acts as the prescriber or medical director while nurses run the infusions.

Alberta LPNs, regulated by the CLPNA, can also perform IV therapy when they hold the additional certification for it. As always, the substance being infused needs a prescriber.

## Naturopathic doctors and IV therapy in Alberta

Alberta allows naturopathic doctors to administer a defined list of intravenous substances when they hold the additional IV authorization from the College of Naturopathic Doctors of Alberta. In practice, the Alberta ND IV scope sits in a narrower window than the Ontario model, so the specific substances an authorized Alberta ND may give are more limited.

The practical point for patients: a naturopathic clinic offering IVs in Alberta should be able to confirm that the treating ND holds CNDA IV authorization. It is reasonable to ask.

## Who cannot legally start your IV in Alberta

- Aestheticians or medical-spa staff who are not also nurses or physicians
- Wellness coaches, nutritionists, or unregulated "IV technicians"
- Naturopaths without CNDA IV authorization
- Anyone performing the injection outside their authorized scope

If a clinic cannot tell you the credential of the person starting your IV, treat that as a reason to pause.

## What to check before you book in Alberta

- **Ask who starts the IV** and their credential (RN, NP, MD, certified LPN, or a CNDA-authorized ND).
- **Ask who prescribes or acts as medical director.**
- **Ask what is in the bag** and whether it is compounded by a licensed pharmacy.
- **Be skeptical of cure or detox claims.** IV vitamin therapy is a legitimate wellness service, not a disease treatment.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a naturopath give you an IV in Alberta?

Yes, but only a naturopathic doctor who holds IV authorization from the College of Naturopathic Doctors of Alberta, and only for a defined list of substances that is narrower than the Ontario ND scope. A naturopath without that authorization cannot legally start an IV. It is reasonable to ask a naturopathic clinic to confirm its CNDA IV authorization before you book.

### Can an LPN give IV therapy in Alberta?

Yes, a licensed practical nurse in Alberta can perform IV therapy when they hold the additional certification for it under the College of Licensed Practical Nurses of Alberta. The substance being infused still needs a prescriber, typically a physician or nurse practitioner.

### Do you need a doctor on site for IV therapy in Alberta?

Not necessarily a physician in the room, but there must be a qualified prescriber behind the treatment. Nurse practitioners can prescribe and oversee protocols themselves, and many Alberta clinics are NP-led. Others use a physician medical director. What matters is that a licensed prescriber stands behind what you receive.

### Is IV vitamin therapy legal in Alberta?

Yes. Wellness IV therapy is legal in Alberta when the restricted activity is performed by an authorized professional using ingredients that are legal to compound and administer. The regulation is about who performs the act and what is in the bag, not a ban on the service.

### What should I ask a clinic before my first IV in Alberta?

Ask who will start the IV and their credential, who prescribes or acts as medical director, what is in the drip and where it is compounded, and how they screen you for contraindications. Clear answers point to a clinic that takes the safety infrastructure seriously.
