NAD+ IV therapy is one of the most searched and most oversold drips on the Canadian wellness menu. Somewhere between the "reverse aging in one session" marketing and the dismissive "it's just an expensive placebo" takes, there is a more useful, honest picture. This guide lays it out: what NAD+ actually is, the dosing tiers Canadian clinics use, why the sessions run so long, what the evidence does and does not support, and what it costs.

This is general information, not medical advice. NAD+ IV therapy is a wellness service, not a treatment for any diagnosed condition, and you should be screened by a licensed clinician before a first infusion. If your symptoms have an underlying medical cause, rule that out first.

## The short answer

NAD+ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a coenzyme your cells use to turn nutrients into energy and to run repair processes. Levels decline with age. NAD+ IV therapy delivers it directly into the bloodstream, bypassing the poor absorption of oral NAD+. People use it for energy, mental clarity, and recovery, and it is used adjunctively in some supervised addiction-recovery programs. The biology is real and plausible, but the human evidence for most wellness claims is still emerging, and the anti-aging promises run well ahead of what has been proven.

## Dosing tiers, and why a NAD+ drip takes hours

The single most important thing to understand about NAD+ IV is that it must be infused slowly. Pushed too fast, it commonly causes flushing, nausea, cramping, and a temporary chest-tightness sensation (usually muscular, not cardiac) that eases the moment the drip is slowed. That is why NAD+ sessions are so much longer than a standard vitamin drip.

Canadian clinics generally work in tiers:

- **Starter (around 250mg):** roughly 2 to 4 hours.
- **Mid-dose (around 500mg):** longer again, often most of an afternoon.
- **High-dose (1000mg and up):** can stretch to 6 to 8 hours.

The long duration is a safety feature, not a sign of a premium product. A good provider starts slow and adjusts the rate to your tolerance rather than racing the bag.

## What the evidence actually says

Here is the honest version. NAD+ is one of the more studied IV interventions, but "more studied" is relative, and much of the human data comes from small studies. The underlying biology, that NAD+ is central to mitochondrial energy production and to DNA-repair enzymes called sirtuins, is well established. What is not established is that raising NAD+ by infusion delivers the broad wellness benefits it is marketed for in otherwise healthy people.

- **Energy and clarity:** many people report feeling better; the controlled evidence is limited.
- **Addiction recovery:** NAD+ protocols are used in some supervised recovery settings, and this is one of the more discussed clinical contexts, though it remains an area of active study rather than settled proof.
- **Anti-aging / longevity:** there is no proof that NAD+ infusions reverse aging in humans. This is where marketing most outpaces evidence.

Approach NAD+ as an emerging therapy with realistic expectations, and be skeptical of any clinic promising guaranteed or dramatic results.

## Is NAD+ IV safe?

NAD+ is generally well tolerated under qualified supervision. Most side effects track directly with infusion speed and ease when the rate is slowed. People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, who have significant heart conditions, or who take multiple medications should be cautious, and a licensed clinician should screen you before a first infusion. Most clinics recommend a series of sessions rather than a single drip, because any effects tend to build gradually.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does NAD+ IV therapy actually work?

Many people report more energy and clearer thinking, but the human evidence is still limited and mostly from small studies. The underlying biology is plausible, yet the general-wellness benefits are not firmly proven. It is best viewed as an emerging therapy approached with realistic expectations, not a guaranteed result.

### Why does a NAD+ drip take so long?

NAD+ has to be infused slowly because giving it quickly triggers flushing, nausea, and chest tightness. Stretching a session to several hours keeps it comfortable and safe. The long duration is a safety feature, not just a marker of a higher dose.

### How much does NAD+ IV cost in Canada?

It depends heavily on dose. In Toronto, published menu prices range from roughly CA$79 for a small add-on push to CA$799 for a high-dose session, with a median around CA$250 based on our Price Index. Higher doses cost more and take longer. Confirm the exact price and dose with the clinic.

### Can NAD+ reverse aging?

No. NAD+ is involved in cellular repair pathways, but there is no proof that infusions reverse aging in humans. Claims to that effect currently outpace the evidence, so treat them as marketing rather than fact.

### Is NAD+ IV safe for everyone?

Generally it is well tolerated under supervision, but it is not for everyone. People who are pregnant or breastfeeding, have significant heart conditions, or take several medications should be cautious, and a licensed clinician should screen you first. Most side effects are mild and tied to how fast the drip runs.
