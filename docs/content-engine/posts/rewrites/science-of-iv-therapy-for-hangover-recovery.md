A hangover feels like dehydration, so a bag of IV fluids sounds like the obvious cure. That intuition built an entire industry. The science underneath it is more interesting, and more limited, than the marketing suggests.

This is the honest version of the hangover IV story: what a hangover actually is, which part a drip genuinely fixes, which parts it cannot touch, and how to decide whether the price makes sense for you. General information, not medical advice.

## What a hangover actually is

Alcohol does several unpleasant things at once. It suppresses a hormone that helps you retain water, so you urinate away more fluid than you take in. Its metabolism produces acetaldehyde, a genuinely toxic intermediate your liver has to clear. It disrupts sleep architecture, irritates the stomach lining, and triggers a mild inflammatory response. Congeners in darker drinks add their own contribution.

The morning-after misery is all of those stacked together. Dehydration is one ingredient, and this matters, because it is the only ingredient a bag of saline addresses.

## What an IV genuinely fixes

Fluids delivered to a vein rehydrate you, and they do it faster than drinking, because nothing has to pass through an irritated stomach. If the drip includes an anti-nausea medication, that component does real, immediate work too, and it is worth noting that it is a prescription drug, which means someone must be [legally prescribing it for you](/blog/how-to-verify-iv-provider-license-canada-2026).

That is the honest win: faster resolution of the dehydration component, plus symptom relief from any actual medications in the bag.

## What it cannot do

No drip removes acetaldehyde faster, repairs the lost sleep, or shortcuts the liver's cleanup timeline. The [University of Rochester Medical Center's review](https://www.urmc.rochester.edu/news/publications/health-matters/hangover-ivs-do-they-really-work) of hangover IVs lands on a one-word verdict: maybe. Their experts note that a drip rehydrates faster than drinking but does not eliminate the root cause, the toxic byproducts of alcohol metabolism, and that for most people water, electrolyte drinks and broth do the job at a fraction of the cost.

In other words, an IV compresses the hydration part of your recovery from hours to minutes, and leaves the rest of the hangover on its original schedule.

## Is it worth the money?

Hangover drips on Canadian menus typically run 150 to 300 dollars. Whether that is worth it is a genuinely personal call, and there are honest reasons to say yes: a wedding day, a flight, a morning where you cannot afford to be horizontal, or simple vomiting that makes drinking fluids impossible. Those are purchases of speed and comfort, and speed and comfort are real things.

What the price does not buy is a cure, and a clinic that uses that word is telling you about its marketing rather than its medicine. It is also fair to say the cheapest effective treatment for most hangovers remains fluids, food, time, and an over-the-counter pain reliever chosen with a little care.

## If you do book one

- Ask what is in the bag, ingredient by ingredient, and who prescribed any medications in it.
- Mention every medication you take and any conditions you have. If anything in our guide to [who should not get IV therapy](/blog/who-should-not-get-iv-therapy-canada-2026) applies to you, that conversation comes first.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a hangover IV take to work?

The fluids run in over 30 to 60 minutes, and the rehydration effect is felt quickly, often before the bag is finished. What it does not accelerate is the clearing of alcohol's metabolic byproducts, so expect to feel better hydrated, not instantly cured.

### How much does a hangover IV cost in Canada?

Published Canadian clinic menus typically price hangover drips between 150 and 300 dollars, with mobile services charging a premium on top for coming to you. Drinking fluids costs approximately nothing, which is the comparison worth keeping in mind.

### Is a hangover IV safe?

For most healthy adults, the main risks are the ordinary ones of any IV: bruising, irritation at the site, and rarely infection or an ingredient reaction. The more important safety questions are who is administering it, who prescribed any medications in it, and whether anyone screened you. People with kidney or heart conditions should talk to their doctor before any infusion.

### Do hangover IVs actually cure hangovers?

No. They treat the dehydration component quickly and can relieve nausea if medication is included, but the fatigue, headache and fog driven by alcohol's metabolic byproducts resolve on their own timeline. The University of Rochester's expert summary of whether they work is, verbatim, maybe.
