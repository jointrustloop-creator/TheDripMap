Can you eat before an IV drip? Yes, and you generally should. Unlike some blood tests or medical procedures, a wellness IV does not require fasting, and arriving on an empty stomach is the most common reason people feel lightheaded or queasy in the chair. This short guide covers what to do before and after a drip: food, water, coffee, alcohol, and exercise, plus the one drip where preparation matters more.

As always, this is general information, not medical advice. If your clinic gives you specific prep instructions, or a clinician has told you to fast for a medical reason, follow their guidance over anything here.

## Before your IV: the short checklist

- **Eat a normal meal one to two hours before.** Food in your stomach steadies your blood sugar and makes lightheadedness, nausea, and that clammy needle-feeling far less likely. There is no benefit to arriving hungry.
- **Drink water beforehand.** Being well hydrated plumps up your veins, which makes the needle placement quicker and more comfortable. A glass or two in the hours before is plenty.
- **Coffee is fine in moderation.** A regular morning coffee will not interfere with a wellness drip. Skip the triple espresso on an empty stomach, mostly for the same queasiness reasons.
- **Skip alcohol before your appointment.** Alcohol dehydrates you, which works directly against the drip and shrinks the veins the nurse needs. For what happens on the other side of the appointment, see our guide on [drinking alcohol after IV therapy](/blog/can-you-drink-alcohol-after-iv-therapy).
- **Wear something with loose sleeves.** The IV usually goes in your forearm or the crook of your elbow.
- **Mention medications and conditions during screening.** A good clinic screens you before your first drip. Bring up anything you take regularly; our guide to [medication interactions](/blog/iv-therapy-medication-interactions-guide) covers why this matters.

## The exception worth knowing: NAD+

Most wellness drips take 30 to 60 minutes. [NAD+ IV therapy](/treatments/nad-plus) is different: it runs slowly, often 2 to 4 hours, because infusing it quickly causes flushing and nausea. Eat a proper meal before a NAD+ session, bring water and something to do, and do not plan it on a day you need to rush. Our [NAD+ in Canada guide](/blog/nad-iv-therapy-canada-dosing-safety-evidence-2026) covers the dosing tiers and timing in detail.

## After your IV: what is normal and what to do

- **Carry on with your day.** No downtime is needed after a standard wellness drip. Most people go straight back to work.
- **Exercise is fine, gently.** There is no medical ban on movement, but many people prefer to keep the first few hours easy. If the insertion site is tender, give it the day.
- **A small bruise at the site is common.** It fades in a few days. Pressure right after the needle comes out keeps it small.
- **Keep drinking water.** A drip is not a substitute for normal hydration habits, whatever the marketing says.
- **Call the clinic if something feels wrong.** Spreading redness, swelling, warmth at the site, or feeling genuinely unwell afterward is a reason to contact the clinic that treated you, not to wait it out.

## How often is too often?

Frequency depends on the drip and your goals, and more is not better. Most clinics suggest spacing wellness drips out rather than stacking them weekly forever. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can you eat before an IV drip?

Yes, and you should. A normal meal one to two hours before your appointment keeps your blood sugar steady and makes lightheadedness or nausea during the drip much less likely. Wellness IVs do not require fasting. Only fast if a clinician has specifically told you to for a medical reason.

### Do you need to fast before IV therapy?

No. Fasting requirements apply to certain blood tests and medical procedures, not to wellness IV drips. Arriving hungry actually makes side effects like dizziness more likely. If a clinic tells you to fast, ask them why so you understand the specific reason.

### Can you drink coffee before an IV drip?

Yes, a normal amount of coffee is fine. Caffeine does not interfere with a wellness drip. The practical advice is simply not to show up on a large dose of caffeine with an empty stomach, which is a recipe for feeling shaky in the chair.

### Should you drink water before an IV?

Yes. Being well hydrated makes your veins easier to access, which means a quicker, more comfortable needle placement. A glass or two of water in the hours before your appointment is enough; there is no need to overdo it.

### Can you exercise after an IV drip?

Yes, there is no required downtime after a standard wellness drip. Many people prefer to keep the first few hours light, and if the insertion site is tender it is sensible to rest that arm for the day. If you notice spreading redness or swelling at the site, contact the clinic before working out.
